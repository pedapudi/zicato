The noise cloud is the reliability headline. Run a fixed champion K times; the run-to-run spread of the scalar IS the measurement noise — the noise floor σ that promote_margin must clear. The figure draws the distribution as a violin and then draws the promote_margin band INSIDE that noise: if the margin sits within the cloud, you are promoting on noise — a challenger "wins" by a margin smaller than the instrument's own jitter. The companion stat is the decision-flip rate: run the whole tournament twice, measure P(the gate decision flips). Two variants: the violin with the margin overlay, and a flip-rate ledger.

Part of the reflection / "Instrument lens" design study — mockups for board-reflection (Measurement-System-Analysis for the evaluation contract). Companion to the board-reflection design note, not a TODO. ← study index.

The sample · K=24 champion self-replications

The same champion run 24 times. Noise floor σ = 0.018; the configured promote_margin = 0.010 sits inside the noise (margin < σ). Re-running the whole tournament flips the gate decision 31% of the time. The recommendation: raise the margin to ≈ 2–3× σ (0.036–0.054). Lower scalar = better.
