2 · Mark — stroke-weight × dot-radius option matrix (6b)

The current mark uses stroke-width 2.4 + accent dot r 3.2 — both go sub-pixel small and the line nearly vanishes at ~16px, reading lighter than the wordmark. The matrix renders the real spiral across a size ladder (16 / 24 / 48 / 96 / 180 px) for each candidate stroke weight. One continuous stroke, single accent, theme-adaptive throughout.

Render-size-aware rule (graded mark variants). Above ~24px the dot is kept and grows gently as the mark shrinks — r(px) = 5.5·(180/px)0.7 — so it stays a real note. At/below 24px the dot is dropped entirely and the stroke thickens (see §3b); a dot that small is just noise. The constant r 5.5 row shows why dropping wins: at 16–24px the fixed dot fades to a whisper.

Compare for (b): read the 16px column — 2.4 is a whisper, 5.0 carries weight kin to the wordmark, 6.5 clogs the inner turns. And ≤24px the accent dot is dropped (noise that small — see §3b) while the stroke thickens; the dot is kept + sized only ≥32px.

3 · Size-threshold swap — full mark vs. bold-z favicon

Even thickened, the full golden spiral muddies into noise below ~24px. The proposal: serve the full mark ≥ 24px and swap to the simplified zicato-favicon.svg bold-z (with the green plucked-note) below ~24px. Here both are shown at 16px and 24px so the crossover is visible.

16 / 24px icons — drop the accent dot, thicken the line

Two small-size icons, two rules. The full spiral mark: at 16–24px its accent dot is noise on an already-muddy glyph — drop the dot, thicken the stroke. The z+note favicon: the bold-z stays legible small, so it keeps its accent dot at every size (the note is the whole point and reads fine — ✓ keep). Net: full mark → drop dot + thicken; z+note → always keep the dot.

Compare for (c): the full spiral is mush at 16px regardless of weight, so below ~24px ship the z+note favicon — which keeps its accent dot (legible at 16px). The accent-dot drop + thicker stroke applies only to the full spiral mark, for the rare case it must render small.
